# Current Situation

As of January 31, 2025, Chasiv Yar is a contested urban battlefield. Russian forces have made confirmed, incremental gains through January along Dniprovska Street [1], Myru and Horkoho streets [2], Tolstoho Street in western Chasiv Yar [3] and Zaliznychna Street in northwestern Chasiv Yar [4], and hold ground within and around the Chasiv Yar Refractory Plant in the center of town, which Russian and Ukrainian forces contested repeatedly through the month. The western part of the settlement is contested rather than securely held by either side: beyond the geolocated Russian advances on Tolstoho and Zaliznychna streets, Russian sources claimed through late January to be clearing Novopivnichnyi Microraion in western Chasiv Yar [5], to have advanced to Tsentralna Street in western Chasiv Yar [6], along Vysokovoltna Street in southwestern Chasiv Yar [1], and toward Mykolaivka west of Chasiv Yar [1] [7]; ISW has not confirmed those claims, and ISW assessed no confirmed advances in the direction on January 31 [7]. Russian sources also repeatedly claimed the plant seized or cleared during January — on January 18 [8], January 21 [9] and January 23 [10] — and ISW did not confirm it; geolocated footage published January 18 indicated that Ukrainian forces had regained lost positions inside the plant [3]. Fighting there continues.

North of the settlement, Ukrainian military observer Kostyantyn Mashovets stated on January 11 that Russian forces had seized Hryhorivka, and ISW assessed that Russian forces had recently advanced in the direction that day [11]; Russian forces nonetheless continued ground attacks north of Chasiv Yar near Hryhorivka on January 17 [12], and a Russian milblogger claimed a further advance near Hryhorivka on January 27 [2]. The Ukrainian General Staff published a map indicating that Russian forces likely advanced along the O-0508 highway in southeastern Orikhovo-Vasylivka north of Chasiv Yar [13]. Russian claims to have seized Pivnichnyi Microraion (northern Chasiv Yar) and Novopivnichnyi Microraion — which ISW places in western Chasiv Yar on January 25 [5] and northwestern Chasiv Yar on January 29 [13] — remain unconfirmed as of January 31 [13] [7]; Mashovets stated on January 18 that Russian forces had seized a foothold in Pivnichnyi Microraion [8]. South of the settlement, geolocated footage published January 28 indicated that Russian forces advanced along the T-0504 Bakhmut–Kostyantynivka highway and westward over part of the waste heap [13], and Russian milbloggers claimed further advances in forested areas south of Chasiv Yar that ISW has not confirmed [1]. A Russian milblogger described Shevchenko Microraion in southwestern Chasiv Yar as a contested "gray" zone [7].

Russian milbloggers claimed on January 11 that Russian forces were bypassing the fortified Refractory Plant rather than reducing it frontally — one claiming that Russian forces were occupying Pivnichnyi Microraion and the railway station to disrupt Ukrainian ground lines of communication, another that attacks in southern Chasiv Yar were intended to bypass the center of the settlement in an attempt to encircle it [11]. ISW reported these as claims and did not adopt them. Daily attacks continue within the settlement itself, north near Novomarkove and Orikhovo-Vasylivka, and south near Stupochky, Bila Hora and Predtechyne [7]. Russian forces conduct small-group infantry assaults daily [6], with motorcycles and other vehicles used to transport personnel [14]; Ukraine's Luhansk Group of Forces Spokesperson Lieutenant Colonel Dmytro Zaporozhets stated on January 27 that Russian forces are increasingly using equipment, especially armored fighting vehicles, in the direction [2]. Ukrainian units reported destroying four armored vehicles while repelling a reduced company-sized mechanized assault [14], four BMDs during a reinforced platoon-sized mechanized assault and eight BMDs damaged or destroyed in total [5], and two tanks and three armored vehicles destroyed or damaged during an attack near Bila Hora [15]. A Russian milblogger claimed that Ukrainian forces counterattacked in southern Chasiv Yar on January 25 [5].

The Russian grouping reportedly operating in the sector is built around the 98th VDV Division (215th Reconnaissance Battalion, 299th and 331st VDV regiments) with the 200th Motorized Rifle Brigade (14th AC, Leningrad Military District) and the 4th Motorized Rifle Brigade (3rd CAA), the 88th "Hispaniola" and "Sever-V" (Russian Volunteer Corps) volunteer brigades, and Chechen "Akhmat" Spetsnaz and "Rusich" elements on the southeastern outskirts [2] [13] [1] [7]. A spokesperson for one Ukrainian brigade operating in the direction stated on January 26 that elements of the 98th VDV Division operating in and near Chasiv Yar are well trained and that Chechen "Akhmat" Spetsnaz elements including former Wagner Group and "Rusich" soldiers are reinforcing the airborne elements, and that Russian forces take advantage of times when Ukrainian forces decrease drone use due to fog and precipitation [6].

# Recent Developments

### January 18, 2025

Russian forces recently advanced in Chasiv Yar on January 18. Geolocated footage published on January 18 shows Ukrainian forces striking two Russian armored vehicles, likely of the Russian 98th Airborne (VDV) Division, near Dniprovska Street in central Chasiv Yar, indicating that Russian forces recently advanced in the settlement. Russian sources claimed that Russian forces seized the Chasiv Yar Refractory Plant, though ISW has not observed visual evidence of this claim. Ukrainian military observer Kostyantyn Mashovets stated that elements of the Russian 4th Motorized Rifle Brigade (3rd CAA, formerly 2nd LNR AC) with support from the 299th and 331st VDV regiments (both of the 98th VDV Division) and the 200th Motorized Rifle Brigade (14th AC, Leningrad Military District) are trying to seize northern Chasiv Yar and that Russian forces seized a foothold in Pivnichnyi Microraion. Drone operators of the Russian "Sever-V" Brigade (Russian Volunteer Corps) are reportedly operating near Chasiv Yar. Tank operators of the Russian "Nevsky" Volunteer Reconnaissance-Assault Brigade (Russian Volunteer Corps) are reportedly operating near Soledar.[8]

### January 19, 2025

Both Russian and Ukrainian forces recently advanced in Chasiv Yar amid continued offensive operations on January 19. Geolocated footage published on January 18 indicates that Ukrainian forces recently regained lost positions in the Refractory Plant in central Chasiv Yar. Geolocated footage published on January 19 indicates that Russian forces recently advanced along Tolstoho Street in western Chasiv Yar. Russian milbloggers claimed on January 19 that Russian forces captured the Novopivnichnyi and Desyata microraions in Chasiv Yar, advanced in the workshop area of the Refractory Plant, and in northern Chasiv Yar. ISW has not observed confirmation of these claims, however. Russian forces continued ground attacks within Chasiv Yar itself and south of Chasiv Yar near Predtechyne, Stupochky, and Bila Hora on January 18 and 19. Ukraine's Luhansk Group of Forces Spokesperson Lieutenant Colonel Dmytro Zaporozhets reported on January 18 that Ukrainian forces repelled Russian small and medium-sized assaults on the grounds of the Refractory Plant in central Chasiv Yar. Elements of the Russian 215th Reconnaissance Battalion (98th VDV Division), drone operators of the Russian "Terek" Cossack Reconnaissance Brigade (reportedly of the volunteer Cossack Assault Corps), and drone operators of the Russian "Sever-V" Brigade (Russian Volunteer Corps) are all reportedly operating in the Chasiv Yar direction; additional elements of the Russian 98th VDV Division and elements of the 200th Motorized Rifle Brigade (14th Army Corps [AC], Leningrad Military District [LMD]) are reportedly operating in Chasiv Yar itself.[3]

# Chronology

### January 7–17, 2025 — Refractory Plant and Pivnichnyi Microraion

Through the first half of January, Russian forces attacked in and around Chasiv Yar every day but converted little of that effort into confirmed ground. ISW assessed in its own voice that Russian forces had recently advanced in the direction on several days, including January 10 [17], January 11 [11] and January 16 [18], but geolocated only two advances in the period: footage published January 9 showing Russian forces in the fields east of Stupochky south of the settlement [17], and footage published January 15 showing Russian forces inside the Chasiv Yar Refractory Plant in central Chasiv Yar [18]. Ukrainian military observer Kostyantyn Mashovets separately stated on January 11 that Russian forces had seized Hryhorivka north of the town [11]. Most other reported gains were Russian claims ISW did not confirm, and the Refractory Plant was the object about which Russian sources most persistently overclaimed: a Russian source claimed on January 10 that Russian forces had advanced to its third and fourth workshops [17]; a Russian milblogger claimed on January 11 that Russian forces occupied about 20 percent of the plant but found it difficult to advance against fortified Ukrainian positions [11], and Russian sources repeated the 20 percent figure on January 12 [19]; milbloggers claimed the seizure of its main part and of its fourth workshop on January 13 [20]; and on January 14 a Russian milblogger claimed Russian forces had pushed Ukrainian forces from the plant, which another milblogger called premature and inaccurate [21]. A further milblogger claimed on January 17 that Russian forces were still fighting for the plant [12]. The same pattern applied to Pivnichnyi Microraion in northern Chasiv Yar, claimed half-taken on January 13 [20] and fully taken on January 16 [18] without confirmation. Alongside the center, Russian forces attacked continuously within the settlement, northeast toward Bondarne and Vasyukivka [22], north near Hryhorivka and Pryvillya [12], and south near Bila Hora, Stupochky, Predtechyne and Oleksandro-Shultyne [21]. Ukraine's Khortytsia Group of Forces stated on January 12 that Russian forces used an unspecified number of armored vehicles to support offensive operations in Chasiv Yar and that Ukrainian forces destroyed one armored vehicle [19].

Two Russian milbloggers claimed on January 11 that the Russian effort was aimed at bypassing the fortified plant rather than reducing it frontally: one claimed that Russian forces were occupying Pivnichnyi Microraion and the railway station to disrupt Ukrainian GLOCs, and another that Russian attacks in southern Chasiv Yar were intended to bypass the central part of the settlement in an attempt to encircle it [11]. ISW reported these as claims and did not adopt them. Method was consistent: the commander of a Ukrainian drone battalion operating in the Kramatorsk (Chasiv Yar) direction stated on January 8 that Russian forces were assaulting in small infantry groups with armored vehicle support, using buggies, and trying to penetrate the Ukrainian rear with sabotage and reconnaissance groups [23]; Ukraine's Luhansk Group of Forces Spokesperson Colonel Dmytro Zaporozhets reported on January 16 that Russian forces were using the pipes of the Siverskyi Donets–Donbas Canal to accumulate forces and move deeper within Chasiv Yar during foggy weather [18]. The offensive appears to have been constrained: a Russian milblogger claimed on January 10 that Ukrainian electronic warfare was preventing constant Russian drone reconnaissance over Chasiv Yar and that constant shelling and close combat were producing friendly-fire incidents, and another Russian milblogger claimed the same day that the Russian military command needed to commit more manpower to seize Chasiv Yar while doubting the frontline command could obtain it [17]. Russian milbloggers claimed Ukrainian counterattacks in Chasiv Yar on January 12 [19], infantry-led and mechanized Ukrainian counterattacks on January 13 [20], and a Ukrainian counterattack near the Siverskyi Donets–Donbas Canal south of Chasiv Yar on January 17 [12]. Milbloggers also claimed on January 13 that Russian forces had cleared most of the Stupky-Holubovskyi forest south of Chasiv Yar, a claim ISW did not confirm [20]. The Russian grouping reportedly operating in the sector by mid-January included the 98th VDV Division with its 215th Reconnaissance Battalion and 217th, 299th and 331st VDV regiments, the 200th Motorized Rifle Brigade (14th AC, Leningrad Military District), the 4th Motorized Rifle Brigade (3rd CAA, formerly 2nd LNR AC), the "Sever-V" Brigade (Russian Volunteer Corps), the 78th "Sever-Akhmat" Motorized Rifle Regiment (42nd MRD, 58th CAA, SMD), and Chechen "Akhmat" elements in southern Chasiv Yar and the forest area to the south [11] [20] [18] [12].

Sources for this period: [22] [23] [17] [11] [19] [20] [21] [18] [12]

# Analysis

### Concealment and Assault Timing

Ukrainian sources consistently report that Russian assaults in the Chasiv Yar sector are timed to conditions that suppress Ukrainian drone coverage. A spokesperson for a Ukrainian brigade operating in the direction stated on January 25 that Russian forces take advantage of foggy weather and decreased Ukrainian drone operations to conduct assaults [5], and a brigade spokesperson stated on January 26 that Russian forces conduct small-group infantry assaults daily, using times when Ukrainian forces decrease drone use due to fog and precipitation [6]; Ukraine's Luhansk Group of Forces Spokesperson Colonel Dmytro Zaporozhets stated that Russian forces leverage the limited visibility of poor weather to accumulate in northern Chasiv Yar and assault the Refractory Plant [9]. Russian forces have also used the pipe infrastructure of the Siverskyi Donets–Donbas Canal to accumulate forces and move deeper into the settlement during foggy weather [18], to transfer personnel [10], and to hide and move around in [6], and later blew up canal pipes in several places to create passages for armored vehicles, assaults and logistics [2]. A Russian milblogger separately complained that Ukrainian electronic warfare prevents constant Russian drone reconnaissance over the settlement [17]. Ukrainian units separately report substantial Russian vehicle losses in repelling mechanized assaults in late January: four armored vehicles destroyed while repelling a reduced company-sized assault [14], four BMDs destroyed during a reinforced platoon-sized assault and eight damaged or destroyed in total [5], and two tanks and three armored vehicles hit by drones during an attack near Bila Hora [15].
