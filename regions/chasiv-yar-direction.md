# Current Situation

As of January 31, 2025, Chasiv Yar is a contested urban battlefield in which Russian forces hold the eastern and central parts of the settlement and are pressing westward and around both flanks, while Ukrainian forces retain the western districts and the approaches toward Kostyantynivka. Russian forces have made confirmed, incremental gains through January along Dniprovska, Myru, Horkoho, Tolstoho and Zaliznychna streets in central and northwestern Chasiv Yar, and hold ground within and around the Chasiv Yar Refractory Plant in the center of town — the single most fought-over object in the sector. Russian sources repeatedly claimed the plant fully cleared during January; ISW has not confirmed those claims, and Ukrainian forces were confirmed to have regained positions inside the plant grounds as late as mid-January. Fighting there continues.

North of the settlement, Russian forces hold Hryhorivka and have confirmed advances along the O-0508 highway in southeastern Orikhovo-Vasylivka; Russian claims to have fully seized the Pivnichnyi and Novopivnichnyi microraions in northern Chasiv Yar remain unconfirmed as of January 31, though Russian forces clearly hold at least footholds there. South of the settlement Russian forces have advanced along the T-0504 Bakhmut–Kostyantynivka highway and over part of the waste heap, and are working through the forested belt and the Siverskyi Donets–Donbas Canal line toward Predtechyne, Stupochky and Bila Hora. Shevchenko Microraion in southwestern Chasiv Yar is described by Russian sources as a contested gray zone.

The operational design is envelopment rather than frontal reduction: Russian forces attack the center to fix Ukrainian defenders while pushing north through the microraions and the railway station area and south through the forests and canal line, aiming at Ukrainian ground lines of communication toward Kostyantynivka. Daily attacks continue within the settlement itself, north near Novomarkove and Orikhovo-Vasylivka, and south near Stupochky, Bila Hora and Predtechyne. Assaults are predominantly small infantry groups, increasingly supported by armored vehicles, motorcycles and light vehicles; Ukrainian units report destroying multiple BMDs and armored vehicles in repeated platoon- to company-sized mechanized assaults in late January. Ukrainian forces continue to counterattack locally, including in southern Chasiv Yar and around the canal.

The Russian grouping is built around the 98th VDV Division (215th Reconnaissance Battalion, 217th, 299th and 331st VDV regiments) with the 200th Motorized Rifle Brigade (14th AC, LMD) in the north, the 4th Motorized Rifle Brigade (3rd CAA) and the 88th "Hispaniola" and "Sever-V" volunteer brigades, and Chechen "Akhmat" Spetsnaz and "Rusich" elements in the south and southeast. Ukrainian commanders assess these formations as comparatively well trained and note that Russian forces time assaults to fog and precipitation, when Ukrainian drone coverage is reduced.

# Recent Developments

### January 18, 2025

Russian forces recently advanced in Chasiv Yar on January 18. Geolocated footage published on January 18 shows Ukrainian forces striking two Russian armored vehicles, likely of the Russian 98th Airborne (VDV) Division, near Dniprovska Street in central Chasiv Yar, indicating that Russian forces recently advanced in the settlement. Russian sources claimed that Russian forces seized the Chasiv Yar Refractory Plant, though ISW has not observed visual evidence of this claim. Drone operators of the Russian "Sever-V" Brigade (Russian Volunteer Corps) are reportedly operating near Chasiv Yar. Tank operators of the Russian "Nevsky" Volunteer Reconnaissance-Assault Brigade (Russian Volunteer Corps) are reportedly operating near Soledar.

# Chronology

### January 7–17, 2025 — The Refractory Plant Grind and the Double Envelopment

Through the first half of January, Russian forces attacked in and around Chasiv Yar every day but converted very little of that effort into confirmed ground. ISW confirmed only two advances in the period: geolocated footage published January 9 showing Russian forces in the fields east of Stupochky south of the settlement, and geolocated footage published January 15 showing Russian forces inside the Chasiv Yar Refractory Plant in central Chasiv Yar. Ukrainian military observer Kostyantyn Mashovets separately reported on January 11 that Russian forces had seized Hryhorivka north of the town. Everything else in the period was claim rather than confirmation, and the Refractory Plant was the object about which Russian sources most persistently overclaimed: milbloggers reported reaching its third and fourth workshops on January 10, holding roughly 20 percent of it on January 11 and again on January 12, seizing its main part on January 13, and driving Ukrainian forces out of it entirely on January 14 — a claim another Russian milblogger immediately called premature and inaccurate, and which the fighting still reported inside the plant on January 17 refuted. The same pattern applied to the Pivnichnyi Microraion in northern Chasiv Yar, claimed half-taken on January 13 and fully taken on January 16 without confirmation. Alongside the center, Russian forces attacked continuously within the settlement, northeast toward Bondarne and Vasyukivka, north near Hryhorivka and Pryvillya, and south near Bila Hora, Stupochky, Predtechyne and Oleksandro-Shultyne. Ukraine's Khortytsia Group of Forces stated on January 12 that Russian forces used an unspecified number of armored vehicles to support offensive operations in Chasiv Yar and that Ukrainian forces destroyed one armored vehicle. [13] [14] [15] [16] [17] [18] [19] [20] [21]

The period established the operational design that governed the rest of the month. Rather than reduce the fortified plant frontally, Russian forces sought to bypass it — pushing north through the Pivnichnyi Microraion and the railway station to threaten Ukrainian ground lines of communication, and simultaneously south through the Stupky-Holubovskyi forest belt and the Siverskyi Donets–Donbas Canal line in an attempt to envelop the settlement. Method was consistent: small infantry assault groups with limited armored and buggy support, sabotage-and-reconnaissance groups probing the Ukrainian rear, and infiltration through the pipes of the Siverskyi Donets–Donbas Canal to accumulate forces and move deeper into the town under cover of fog, as Ukraine's Luhansk Group of Forces reported on January 16. The offensive appears to have been constrained: a Russian milblogger claimed on January 10 that Ukrainian electronic warfare was preventing constant Russian drone reconnaissance over Chasiv Yar and that shelling and close combat were producing friendly-fire incidents; Ukrainian forces held fortified positions in the plant, and Russian milbloggers claimed Ukrainian infantry-led and mechanized counterattacks on January 12, 13 and 17; and Russian milbloggers themselves argued that seizing Chasiv Yar would require more manpower than the frontline command was likely to obtain. The Russian grouping reported in the sector by mid-January included the 98th VDV Division with its 215th Reconnaissance Battalion and 217th, 299th and 331st regiments, the 200th Motorized Rifle Brigade, the 4th Motorized Rifle Brigade, the "Sever-V" Brigade, the 78th "Sever-Akhmat" Motorized Rifle Regiment (42nd MRD, 58th CAA, SMD), and Chechen "Akhmat" elements in the south. # Analysis

### Concealment as an Enabler of Assault Tempo

The Chasiv Yar fight is governed less by mass than by visibility. Russian assaults cluster in conditions that suppress Ukrainian drone coverage — fog, precipitation, low light — and Russian forces have made systematic use of the Siverskyi Donets–Donbas Canal's pipe infrastructure to move and concentrate personnel out of aerial view before emerging inside or near the settlement. The reciprocal is equally consistent: where Ukrainian drone and electronic-warfare coverage holds, mechanized assaults are broken up with heavy vehicle losses, and Russian milbloggers have complained that Ukrainian electronic warfare denies them persistent aerial reconnaissance over the settlement. Concealment, not combat power, is the variable that determines whether a given Russian push in this sector converts into terrain. [15] [20]
