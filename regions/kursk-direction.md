# Current Situation

Russian forces intensified offensive operations in Kursk Oblast on January 9, conducting a reinforced battalion-sized mechanized assault northwest of Sudzha. Geolocated footage published on January 9 indicates Russian forces seized Leonidovo and Aleksandriya and advanced northwest of Nikolskyi and west of Vikotorovka (all northwest of Sudzha) during the assault. A Ukrainian brigade operating in Kursk Oblast reported that Russian forces conducted the assault in six waves using 50 tanks, IFVs, APCs, and civilian vehicles, and that Ukrainian forces destroyed an unspecified number of Russian vehicles during the assault. Additional geolocated footage published on January 8 and 9 indicates Russian forces advanced in Pogrebki (northeast of Sudzha) and marginally advanced in Makhnovka (east of Sudzha). Russian milbloggers claimed Russian forces seized Pogrebki and advanced further south, but ISW has not observed confirmation of these maximalist claims. Russian forces reintensified assaults in Kursk Oblast on January 5 and have conducted assaults throughout the salient over the last four days after mainly focusing east of Sudzha in recent months.[1]

Ukrainian forces recently advanced northwest of Sudzha. Geolocated footage published on January 9 indicates Ukrainian forces recently advanced in western Nikolayevka (further northwest of Sudzha). Russian milbloggers claimed Ukrainian forces are counterattacking near Pogrebki, Novaya Sorochina, Staraya Sorochina, and Nikolayevka.[1]

President Volodymyr Zelensky stated on January 9 that 4,000 North Korean military personnel have been killed or wounded during the war to date, presumably in Kursk Oblast -- indicating Ukrainian forces have killed or wounded roughly one-third of the roughly 12,000 North Korean troops deployed to Russia in 2024. Zelensky stated in late December 2024 that over 3,000 North Korean personnel had been killed or wounded.[1]

Elements of the Russian 106th Airborne (VDV) Division are reportedly operating near Sverdlikovo (east of Sudzha). Elements of the Russian 30th Motorized Rifle Regiment (72nd MRD, 44th AC, Leningrad Military District) and 2nd Motorized Rifle Regiment are reportedly operating near Novoivanovka, Sverdlikovo, and other areas.[1]

Russian forces continued offensive operations in the Ukrainian salient in Kursk Oblast on January 10. Geolocated footage published on January 9 and 10 shows that Russian forces marginally advanced within southern Makhnovka (east of Sudzha) and advanced in southern Russkoye Porechnoye and northern Kositsa (both northeast of Sudzha). A Russian milblogger claimed that Russian forces encircled some Ukrainian positions near Guyevo and Kurilivka (both south of Sudzha). A Russian milblogger continued to claim that elements of the Russian 30th Motorized Rifle Regiment (72nd MRD, 44th AC, Leningrad Military District) and 34th Motorized Rifle Brigade (49th CAA, Southern Military District) seized Pogrebki (north of Sudzha), but other Russian milbloggers refuted this claim. Russian milbloggers widely refuted claims that Russian forces encircled Malaya Loknya (north of Sudzha) and entered Sudzha. Russian forces continued attacking throughout the salient, including near Sudzha itself; northwest of Sudzha near Novaya Sorochina, Staraya Sorochina, Nikolayevka, Viktorovka, and Nikolskiy; northeast of Sudzha near Cherkasskoye Porechnoye and Russkoye Porechnoye; and east of Sudzha near Zamoste and Makhnovka on January 9 and 10. A Russian milblogger claimed that Russian forces are focusing their main efforts on the area northwest of Sudzha from positions west of Novoivanovka, through Leonidovo and Alexandriya, and between Lebedevka and Malaya Loknya towards the Sudzha-Lgov highway. Russian milbloggers claimed that Ukrainian forces counterattacked near Berdin (northeast of Sudzha) and in Kruglenkoye (northwest of Sudzha). Drone elements of the Russian 810th Naval Infantry Brigade (Black Sea Fleet, SMD) reportedly continue to operate in the Kursk direction.[2]
