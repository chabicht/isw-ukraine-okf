# Background

The Institute for the Study of War (ISW) is a Washington, DC-based non-profit think tank that produces detailed daily assessments of the Russia-Ukraine war through its Russian Offensive Campaign Assessment series. ISW does not receive classified material and relies entirely on publicly available information, drawing extensively on Russian, Ukrainian, and Western reporting, social media, commercially available satellite imagery, and geospatial data.[1]

ISW's daily assessments follow a fixed structure: key takeaways, an interactive map linkout, then recurring operational sections covering Ukrainian operations in Russia, the three Russian subordinate main efforts in eastern Ukraine, the southern axis, the air/missile/drone campaign, mobilization and force generation efforts, technological adaptations, occupied-area activities, and Belarus.

# Analysis

ISW's analytical approach emphasizes assessed control of terrain, confirmed advances through geolocated evidence, and distinguishing between Russian claims and verified changes. The daily products maintain a clear separation between observed fact (geolocated footage, official reports) and milblogger claims that lack confirmation. ISW rates Russian claims against its own assessment framework, noting when advances have been confirmed versus merely asserted.

ISW's recurring coverage areas include frontline dynamics across all axes, Russian force generation and mobilization, strike campaigns, Russian technological adaptation, and Kremlin political and economic posturing. The daily assessments serve as a structured, near-real-time record of the war's evolution.
