# Current Situation

Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan and Russian President Vladimir Putin held a phone call on January 17, 2025, indicating growing Kremlin concerns over Armenia's deepening ties with the West. Pashinyan and Putin discussed the results of Armenia's 2024 presidency of the Russian-led Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU), upcoming EAEU activities, and unspecified bilateral issues. The Kremlin claimed that Putin and Pashinyan also addressed deepening integration within the EAEU and highlighted how the EAEU benefits the Armenian economy. The Kremlin claimed that Pashinyan explained Armenia's "recent actions" regarding its relations with the European Union (EU) — likely referring to the Armenian government's January 9 approval of an EU accession bill — although the Armenian readout did not mention this.[1]

The Pashinyan-Putin conversation came shortly after the January 14 launch of the Armenia-US Strategic Partnership, which sparked speculation in Russian information space about Armenia distancing itself and possibly withdrawing from the EAEU — a claim Armenian officials have denied. Putin's emphasis on EAEU cooperation and acknowledgment of EU-Armenia relations during the call likely suggests that the Kremlin is increasingly concerned over Armenia's growing ties with the West, and Putin likely seeks to at least symbolically reassert Russian influence in Armenia to save face.[1]
