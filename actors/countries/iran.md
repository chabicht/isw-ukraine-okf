# Current Situation

Russian President Vladimir Putin and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian signed the Russia-Iran Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Agreement in Moscow on January 17, 2025. The agreement addresses enhanced defense cooperation, intelligence sharing, nuclear energy use and cooperation, transportation support and development of the International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC), and a clause stipulating mutual prohibition on allowing third parties to use their territory to threaten the other's security. The agreement includes unspecified development of "military-technical cooperation" between the two countries.[1]

The bilateral agreement could lay the groundwork for Russia to establish aircraft refueling hubs and a naval presence in Iran, particularly as the fall of Bashar al-Assad's regime and waning Russian influence in the Middle East threatens Russian bases in Syria. Russia could use Iranian territory to support operations in North Africa and the Middle East, though Iran's geographic location is suboptimal compared to Syria's proximity to Libya and the Mediterranean. Russia may use the agreement to establish a more permanent military presence in Iran in the long term, but Iran may be reluctant due to potential additional Western sanctions and retaliation.[1]

The agreement notably lacks a mutual defense clause, indicating that Russia likely lacks the bandwidth to support significant operations outside of Ukraine and is prioritizing its manpower needs through its mutual defense treaty with North Korea. Russia appears to value its ability to mitigate manpower constraints through the North Korea mutual defense clause over further military-technical cooperation with Iran.[1]

Iran has supplied Russia with Shahed drones throughout the war. Ukrainian President Zelensky reported that Ukrainian forces shot down 7,800 Iranian Shahed drones in 2024 alone.
