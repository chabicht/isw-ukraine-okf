# Current Situation

Belarus assumed the presidency of the Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU) on January 1, 2025, a rotating position it will hold until January 1, 2026.[1]

Belarus continues to host Russian forces and support Russian military operations against Ukraine. The country has served as a staging ground for Russian troops and equipment throughout the war, including during the initial invasion in February 2022 and subsequent force generation efforts. Russian missile and drone strikes against Ukraine have been launched from Belarusian territory.

The Belarusian Ministry of Defense announced the start of joint Russian-Belarusian command-staff exercises within the framework of the Union State on January 14, ahead of the anticipated "Zapad-2025" joint military exercises. The exercises will focus on determining when to deploy the Union State Regional Grouping of Forces and will conclude on January 16.[2]

# Analysis

### Russia's De Facto Annexation of Belarus

ISW assesses that the Kremlin is in the endgame of a decades-long strategic effort to de facto annex Belarus through the Union State framework — an effort that will permanently augment Russia's military and economic capabilities to pursue its revanchist geopolitical objectives against the United States and NATO.[3] Moscow's effort, while incomplete, has already achieved significant gains that require NATO to reevaluate the implications of Russia's growing control over Belarus.[3]

### Political Integration

The Kremlin seeks to formalize the Union State as a Russian-dominated federated government granting Moscow dominant power over most aspects of Belarusian governance, including a Kremlin-dominated federated government with a common set of federal laws and institutions for Belarusians and Russians to be governed as a single polity.[3] Russia's political control effort is a whole-of-government undertaking spanning military, political, and economic lines of effort simultaneously.[3]

### Economic Integration

Russia is pursuing a fully integrated economy with Belarus complete with common markets, free labor flows, unified laws, and a currency union.[3] The Kremlin plans to leverage Belarus's population of 9.155 million people and its economic resources in service of Russian state power.[3] These economic integration measures would allow Russia to offset costs from its protracted war against Ukraine and accelerate post-war recovery.[3]

### Strategic Implications

ISW assesses that Russia does not need to make any additional integration gains in Belarus to threaten the West — the existing suite of military, political, and economic integration gains since 2020 already pose security threats to the United States, NATO, and Ukraine, and an existential threat to Belarus's continued existence as a sovereign state.[3] Belarus is not merely a Russian-aligned ally; the Kremlin is transforming Belarus into a strategic enabler for Russia's ability to project power globally, leveraging its geostrategic territory on NATO's eastern flank.[3] Policymakers must start planning for a future in which Belarus is not only a captive nation but also effectively an extension of the Russian Federation.[3]
