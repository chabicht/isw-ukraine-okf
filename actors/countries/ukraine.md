# Current Situation

Ukraine chose not to renew its contract for Russian gas transit through its territory, ending five decades of flow as of January 1, 2025. The decision deprives Russia of significant gas revenue while Ukraine pivots toward European energy integration.[1]

President Volodymyr Zelensky signaled in his New Year's Eve address that Ukraine will increase drone and missile strikes against Russia in 2025. Ukraine produced over one million drones in 2024 and is building domestic missile systems including Palyanytsya, Peklo, Ruta, Neptune, and Sapsan. Zelensky described these weapons as "arguments for a just peace," emphasizing that Ukraine's defense industrial base produced 30 percent of all military equipment used on the frontlines in 2024.[1]

On January 2, Ukrainian forces conducted a missile strike — likely with HIMARS — against a Russian military command post of the 810th Naval Infantry Brigade in Ivanovskoye, Kursk Oblast. The strike hit during an award ceremony at the local House of Culture.[1]

The GUR specified new details about the December 31 naval drone strike in the Black Sea, confirming that a Magura V5 naval drone used missiles to destroy two Russian Mi-8 helicopters and damage a third. Ukrainian strikes against occupied Crimea have degraded Russian logistics, including damage to the railway on the Kerch Strait Bridge and ferry operations.[1]

Recently declassified US documents highlight the integral role of US monetary and technical assistance in expanding Ukraine's domestic drone production capabilities. The US began investing in Ukraine's drone production initiative in Fall 2022 and accelerated these efforts in Summer 2023, enabling Kyiv to rapidly expand its domestic drone production capabilities. US officials stated that US aid helped Ukraine develop a new generation of drones and revolutionize how wars are fought. US support for Ukraine's drone production has yielded insights for American defense industrial companies.[2]

Ukraine continued to offer solutions to Transnistria's energy crisis on January 25. President Zelensky stated during a press conference with Moldovan President Maia Sandu that Ukraine can supply Transnistria with coal at low prices or even free of charge if Transnistria would supply Ukraine with electricity in return, and offered to send specialists to increase the Transnistrian power plant's output.[3]

The US military reportedly transferred roughly 90 decommissioned Patriot missiles from storage facilities in Israel to Poland and is expected to deliver these missiles to Ukraine on an unspecified date, according to US defense officials.[4]

The United Kingdom, Finland, and Czechia announced several immediate and longer-term military assistance packages for Ukraine on January 31. Ukrainian Defense Minister Rustem Umerov stated that the UK will provide Ukraine with a military assistance package valued at two billion GBP (about $2.5 billion), primarily for the purchase of air defense systems and funding for localization of defense production in Ukraine. Finnish Defense Minister Antti Hakkanen announced Finland will provide Ukraine with a new tranche of military assistance valued at almost 200 million euros (about $207 million). Czech Foreign Minister Jan Lipavsky stated that the Czech government is considering creating a new initiative to purchase artillery ammunition for Ukraine.
