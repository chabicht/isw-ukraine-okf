Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant occupation director Yuriy Chernichuk announced on January 1, 2025 that Russia intends to obtain licenses from Moscow to operate all six ZNPP reactors by 2028, starting with the first reactor by the end of 2025. Plans include replacing foreign-sourced equipment with Russian-made equipment and using the plant to supply energy to occupied Ukraine and Russian regions west of the Ural Mountains. Ukrainian licenses for the first ZNPP reactor will expire in December 2025.[1]
