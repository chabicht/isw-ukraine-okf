# Background

Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine fundamentally shifted and intensified the Russo-Iranian relationship. Tehran has leveraged Moscow's growing material and financial requirements to sustain its war effort and support Tehran's own domestic and foreign policy objectives.[1]

# Key Dynamics

### Shared Ideological Core

The core of the Russo-Iranian relationship is a mutually binding interest in challenging and eventually overturning the US-led world order. This shared ideological core allowed the relationship to weather and survive tensions that have arisen since 2022, and the United States should not expect this ideological core to weaken in the years ahead.[1]

### Seven Axes of Cooperation

Russo-Iranian cooperation occurs along seven major axes that relate to and overlap in the defense, economic, and political spheres. The interrelated nature of these nodes of cooperation emphasizes that the success of Russia cannot be separated from the success of Iran.[1]

### Asymmetric Outcomes

The relationship is not perfectly one-to-one. Moscow and Tehran are seeking different outcomes from their collaboration. Russia prioritizes immediate military material needs for the war in Ukraine, while Iran leverages the relationship for broader regional and domestic objectives.[1]

# Current Situation

Russian President Vladimir Putin and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian signed the Russia-Iran Comprehensive Strategic Partnership Agreement in Moscow on January 17, 2025, marking the most formal expression of the deepening bilateral relationship. The agreement addresses enhanced defense cooperation, intelligence sharing, nuclear energy use and cooperation, transportation support and development of the International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC), and unspecified "military-technical cooperation." The agreement notably lacks a mutual defense clause, indicating that Russia prioritizes its manpower needs through its mutual defense treaty with North Korea over further military-technical cooperation with Iran.[2]

Iran has supplied Russia with Shahed drones throughout the war. Ukrainian President Zelensky reported that Ukrainian forces shot down 7,800 Iranian Shahed drones in 2024 alone.[3]

# Analysis

### Defense Cooperation

Iran's provision of Shahed drones and potential ballistic missile transfers to Russia forms the core of defense cooperation. Iran has also assisted Russia in establishing domestic Shahed production capabilities. In return, Russia has provided Iran with advanced military technology, including possible Su-35 fighter aircraft and S-400 air defense systems, though deliveries remain unconfirmed.

### Economic Integration

The International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC) serves as a key economic framework for Russo-Iranian cooperation, offering an alternative trade route that bypasses Western sanctions. Both countries are deepening energy coordination, with Iran serving as a potential hub for Russian energy exports.

### Political Coordination

Russia and Iran coordinate closely on Syria policy, countering US and Western influence in the Middle East, and challenging sanctions regimes. Both countries have aligned positions in multilateral forums including the UN Security Council.
