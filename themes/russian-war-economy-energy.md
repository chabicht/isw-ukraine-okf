# Current Situation

Ukraine's decision to not renew its contract to transport Russian gas through Ukrainian territory, effective January 1, 2025, will cost Gazprom an estimated $6 billion per year in lost gas revenues. Russia's share of the European gas market has dropped from 45 percent in 2021 to approximately 5 percent. The only remaining route to export gas to Europe is the TurkStream pipeline bypassing Ukraine through the Black Sea to Turkey. Slovakia and Austria, the final destinations of the blocked gas, have both met their energy needs through alternative sources.

Gazprom cut gas supplies to Transnistria on January 1, claiming Moldova owed $709 million, though an audit by British and Norwegian firms in 2022 found the actual debt was only $8.6 million. The Kremlin's posturing mirrors its efforts to coerce Europe into authorizing Nord Stream 2 in Winter 2021–2022, likely aiming to extract economic or diplomatic concessions and undermine EU unity.[1]
