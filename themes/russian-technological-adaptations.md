# Current Situation

Recently examined Russian Shahed drones contain Chinese-manufactured satellite navigation Controlled Reception Pattern Antennas (CRPA) that increase resistance to Ukrainian electronic warfare. The Chinese components are reportedly cheaper than similar Iranian- and Russian-produced alternatives.

Russian forces completed a successful second test of a new domestically produced naval drone model capable of carrying a 150-kilogram payload.

Russian primary tank manufacturer Uralvagonzavod reported that a recent batch of T-90M Proryv and modernized T-72B3 tanks contained over 100 modifications compared to pre-2022 models, including anti-drone nets, reinforced rubber armor, additional protection on the stern and engine compartments, integrated electronic warfare systems, and improved camouflage.[1]

Russian forces are increasing the use of fiber optic cable drones in the Toretsk direction that are resistant to Ukrainian EW countermeasures. The drones currently have a range of over 10 kilometers, and Russian forces are improving and extending their range.[2]

A Russian milblogger claimed that the Russian military has failed to modernize its naval drones as Ukrainian forces continue to successfully innovate their naval drone fleet. The milblogger questioned why Russia has not widely deployed various naval drone prototypes despite long development. Russia plans to develop a manufacturing hub for naval drones at the Kingisepp Machine-Building Plant in St. Petersburg in 2025.[3]

Russian forces are adapting older tank models, such as the T-62, to serve as modified armored personnel carriers. A milblogger posted an image of a modified T-62 tank with a flat platform, characterizing the adaptation as an attempt to solve a chronic shortage of Russian APCs.[3]
