# Current Situation

Russian sources expressed widespread concern about the Russian military's ability to react to Ukraine's ongoing combined arms efforts integrating electronic warfare and long-range strike capabilities with ground operations. Ukrainian EW interference during Ukrainian assaults in Kursk Oblast on January 5 prevented Russian forces from operating drones in the area, degrading Russian forces' ability to defend against Ukrainian mechanized attacks. Russian milbloggers noted that drones with fiber optic cables are one of the few Russian drone variants that consistently resist Ukrainian EW countermeasures, though some Russian forces were able to use some FPV and Lancet drones. Russian officials claimed that Russian forces downed three unspecified Ukrainian missiles over Kursk Oblast, suggesting Ukrainian forces may be attempting to integrate longer-range strike capabilities with ground operations and tactical EW systems. Ukrainian forces conducted HIMARS strikes near Bolshoye Soldatskoye and other areas in Kursk Oblast to interdict Russian reinforcements, artillery, and drone operators. These reports indicate that Ukrainian forces in Kursk are employing more effective combined arms tactics, and that Russian forces may be struggling to quickly adapt to Ukrainian battlefield innovations.[1]

Recently examined Russian Shahed drones contain Chinese-manufactured satellite navigation Controlled Reception Pattern Antennas (CRPA) that increase resistance to Ukrainian electronic warfare. The Chinese components are reportedly cheaper than similar Iranian- and Russian-produced alternatives.[2]

Russian forces completed a successful second test of a new domestically produced naval drone model capable of carrying a 150-kilogram payload.[2]

Russian primary tank manufacturer Uralvagonzavod reported that a recent batch of T-90M Proryv and modernized T-72B3 tanks contained over 100 modifications compared to pre-2022 models, including anti-drone nets, reinforced rubber armor, additional protection on the stern and engine compartments, integrated electronic warfare systems, and improved camouflage.[2]

Russian forces are increasing the use of fiber optic cable drones in the Toretsk direction that are resistant to Ukrainian EW countermeasures. The drones currently have a range of over 10 kilometers, and Russian forces are improving and extending their range.[3]

A Russian milblogger claimed that the Russian military has failed to modernize its naval drones as Ukrainian forces continue to successfully innovate their naval drone fleet. The milblogger questioned why Russia has not widely deployed various naval drone prototypes despite long development. Russia plans to develop a manufacturing hub for naval drones at the Kingisepp Machine-Building Plant in St. Petersburg in 2025.[4]

A Russian milblogger claimed on January 10 that Russian forces may not be fielding more advanced naval drones because the Russian Ministry of Defense refuses to fund the development of naval drones.[6]

Russian forces are adapting older tank models, such as the T-62, to serve as modified armored personnel carriers. A milblogger posted an image of a modified T-62 tank with a flat platform, characterizing the adaptation as an attempt to solve a chronic shortage of Russian APCs.[4]

Russian forces are increasingly using drones attached to fiber optic cables that are resistant to electronic warfare interference in strikes against Ukrainian forces throughout the frontline. The spokesperson of a Ukrainian brigade reported on January 8 that Russian forces are not yet systematically employing fiber-optic drones and noted that fiber-optic drones are slower and less maneuverable than FPV drones, making them more vulnerable to small-arms fire. A Russian milblogger claimed on January 8 that Russian forces were the first to successfully develop and integrate fiber-optic drones into combat operations in Ukraine but cautioned that Ukrainian forces would soon develop similar innovations. The milblogger added that Russian forces will need to develop new means of downing Ukrainian fiber-optic drones.[5]
